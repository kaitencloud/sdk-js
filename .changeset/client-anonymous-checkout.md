`KaitenAnonymousCheckoutClient`: what a browser does for a visitor the vendor's backend does not know yet, who buys a self-serve plan from the vendor's own pages with no account, through the `/public/checkout-sessions` routes of Kaiten servers that ship billing.

- `new KaitenAnonymousCheckoutClient({ apiUrl, publishableKey })` takes a publishable key that has anonymous checkout turned on. A class of its own, apart from `KaitenClient` and `KaitenSessionClient`: it holds two credentials of two classes, and a host that offers no checkout does not ship its code.
- `startCheckout` (`POST /public/checkout-sessions`) takes a Turnstile token, the plan by `priceId` or by `licenseSlug` with a `billingPeriod`, add-ons, a voucher code, the buyer's `email`, a `returnUrl`, a `clientReferenceId` and `dryRun`. It answers a `preview` of what is due, or an `open` session. `getCheckoutSession` reads a session and `confirmCheckout` confirms it: it completes at once when nothing has to be paid (a free plan, a trial with no payment method) and otherwise answers `requires_payment_method` with the hosted `setupUrl`, then `awaiting_payment` and `completed`. Money is the server's, in the SDK's conventions (`currencyCode`, integer minor units).
- The credential of a session is its `ksc_` token, a third class beside the publishable key (`pk_`) and the customer session (`kst_`): `isCheckoutSessionToken` and `CHECKOUT_SESSION_TOKEN_PREFIX` tell it apart. The publishable key is sent in `X-Kaiten-Publishable-Key` to the start only, and the token in `Authorization: Bearer ksc_…` to its own session's two routes only; neither is sent with the other, whatever `defaultHeaders` holds, and no cookie is sent. The client keeps the token, which `startCheckout` does not return, in memory and in `sessionStorage`, because the payment method setup is a full-page redirect; never in a URL or in `localStorage`. It drops it once the session closes or the API refuses it.
- `readCheckoutReturn(url?)` reads `kaiten_checkout_session` from the URL the hosted page returns to, and gives the URL without it. `holdsCheckoutSession` says whether the client can still read and confirm a session, and a call on a session it holds no token for rejects with `CheckoutTokenMissingError` before any request.
- A Turnstile token is single-use, a `dryRun` included, so an input the API would refuse for its shape (no token, both forms of the price, no e-mail or return URL on a real start) rejects with a `TypeError` before anything is sent. A read is sent again after a network failure or a 5xx; the start and the confirm never are.
- Four refusals have a class of their own, all `KaitenError`s: `TurnstileFailedError` (422 `*.TurnstileFailed`), `TurnstileUnavailableError` (503 `*.TurnstileUnavailable`, with `retryAfterSeconds`), `CheckoutSessionClosedError` (409 `*.SessionClosed`, with `sessionStatus`) and `DueTodayChangedError` (409 `*.DueTodayChanged`). The 429s of the start and the confirm are `RateLimitedError`s and the provider's 503 a `ProviderUnavailableError`, as for the other public routes.
- `KaitenAnonymousCheckoutClientLike` is its interface. `KaitenClientLike` gains no member, so 1.0 doubles stay valid.
- The catalogue maps what the key can do: `catalog.capabilities.anonymousCheckout` and `turnstileSiteKey`, the site key of the Turnstile widget. They go together (`anonymousCheckout` is true only with a site key) and are `false` and `null` on a server that predates the feature. `BillingCapabilities` gains the two as optional members.
- Bundle: the four refusals above are read by every client of the package, which grows by about 130 bytes gzipped; the anonymous checkout client is 9 174 bytes gzipped on its own.
